Hi! I'm Shiv, a mathematics/statistics geek with an interest in uncovering the mysteries of the universe—well, at least the ones that can be uncovered with math and science.

I started this blog to do some thinking out loud about the various ideas, tidbits, and discussions that occur to me from time to time, or that I come across in my reading. Sometimes it will be math, sometimes it will be something else entirely.

## Thinking out loud

I hope to eventually turn this into a collection of interesting posts, observations, and ideas. You might stumble across a post with an interesting mathematical proof, or an observation about philosophy, or something else entirely.

I'm interested in anything that touches on math, statistics, and their relationships to everything else. If I had to describe the scope in one line:

$$
\text{Inquiry} = \{\, Q \mid Q \text{ touches on math, statistics, or nature} \,\}
$$

## Between rigorous proofs and philosophical musings

Mathematics and statistics give us some of the sharpest lenses ever devised for viewing reality. From the geometry of high-dimensional spaces to the nuances of stochastic inference, formal tools reveal patterns that intuition alone frequently misses.

Yet math is never completely isolated from broader questions about epistemology, cognition, and philosophy. This space exists at that intersection.

## The lifecycle of an idea

This is not meant to be a formal repository of my work, but a place to have ideas, think about them, and eventually write about them. Some ideas will grow into longer posts, some will wait for further development, and others will be abandoned as I move on.

> **Definition (Think-Out-Loud Space).** A dynamic intellectual notebook where ideas are tested informally, proofs are explored for the joy of discovery, and unfinished questions are welcome.

## Questions that don't have answers

In mathematics, we are taught to seek closed forms, decisive Q.E.D.s, and conclusive proofs. There is immense beauty in that clarity.

After all, there's nothing wrong with asking questions that have answers, but there is something fascinating about the far more numerous class of questions that don't have them.
